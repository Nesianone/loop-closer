The Concept

Only one Loop is ever active

A Loop is anything with more than one step — a project, a course, a renovation. A Task is a quick one-off (renew your rego, reply to an email) that lives on your Today screen and doesn't need any of this ceremony.

The app's entire personality comes from one rule: you can only have one Loop active at a time. Everything else you're not actively doing right now has to be honestly labelled as one of two things:

  • Parked — paused, with a note on exactly where you left off, so restarting it later doesn't cost you the "where was I?" tax.
  • Killed — you're not doing it, and you've said why.

Starting something new is deliberately blocked until you've dealt with what's currently active. That friction is the whole point — it's there to stop the pattern of quietly abandoning things to chase the next idea.

First Launch

Getting started

The first time you open the app, it asks you to dump everything you've started and not finished — courses, projects, chores, all of it. Add them one at a time.

Once you're done adding, it walks you through each one and won't let you move on until you've decided what it is:

  1. Active — only one item can be this. It needs a next physical step and a coping plan ("if I get stuck on X, I'll do Y").
  2. Parked — needs a note on where you left off.
  3. Killed — needs a reason (lost interest, wasn't the right idea, hit a wall, or other).

Nothing gets left undecided. Once every item has a home, you land on the Today screen.

Your daily rhythm

Tap Mark done and it immediately asks what tomorrow's step is — you can't leave the screen without deciding, so you're never staring at a stale "next step" from three days ago. Tap Not today if you didn't get to it; that's an honest answer, not a failure.

Underneath, a Quick tasks list handles the small one-off stuff that doesn't deserve full Loop treatment. If a task sits open for two weeks, it gently asks "is this actually a Loop?" — a nudge, not a block.

The streak counter just counts consecutive days you've checked in. No badges, no points — just a number that goes up when you show up.

The Safety Net

When you stall

Miss two check-ins in a row on your active Loop, and next time you open the app it stops you with one question: are you stuck, or are you losing interest? You can't dismiss it — you have to answer.

  • Stuck — it asks you to shrink the next step into something you genuinely can't fail to do.
  • Losing interest — it makes you Park or Kill it, right there. No silent drifting.
Why it's like this

Most habit apps let a stalled project fade quietly. This one treats two missed days as a decision point on purpose — it's much easier to be honest about losing interest on day 3 than to admit it six months later.

The Payoff

Finishing something

When your active Loop is actually done, tap Ship it on the Today screen. It asks one nudge question first — "would you ship this today, at B-minus quality?" — to stop you polishing forever, then asks why finishing it matters to you. Answer both, and it's marked Done, freeing you up to activate something else.

The Full Picture

Everything else, tracked

The Loops tab lists every Loop you've ever had, in any state, filterable by status and by domain (Career, Financial, Home, Health, Community — editable in Settings). This is also where you can:

  • Start a brand new Loop — as active (if nothing else is) or straight to parked.
  • Reactivate a parked Loop — with a fresh (or reused) coping plan.

Each Loop also keeps a quiet running log of every step it's ever had as its "next step" — so a multi-stage project like a renovation doesn't just show its current step, it shows the whole trail once you tap to expand it.

Staying Focused

The idea parking lot

Get a new idea while you're mid-Loop? Don't act on it — capture it in Ideas with a title and an optional note, one tap. It's saved, but it can't hijack your attention: ideas parked here can only ever become an active Loop during your Weekly Review, never on the spot.

Once A Week

The weekly reset

On the day you choose (Sunday by default), a banner appears on Today: Weekly Review is due. Opening it shows your active Loop's progress, every Loop you've killed recently (useful for spotting patterns — do you keep quitting for the same reason?), and your Idea Parking Lot.

That middle row is deliberate: simply parking your current Loop doesn't unlock a shiny new one from the Parking Lot. You have to actually resolve it. Finish reviewing and it logs a permanent entry in Recap — a scrollable, never-edited history of every week, so months from now you have a real record instead of a fuzzy memory.

Housekeeping

Settings & your data

In Settings you can edit your domain tags, change which day the Weekly Review lands on, and — importantly — export a JSON backup of everything.

Back this up

Everything lives only in this phone's browser storage — there's no account, no cloud sync. If you switch phones, clear your browser data, or just want peace of mind, export a backup from Settings every so often. Importing it (here or on a new device) restores everything exactly as it was.

None of this is trying to be a normal productivity app. It's built around one specific, slightly annoying idea: switching should cost you something, and finishing should be visible. If a screen ever feels like it's slowing you down on purpose — it is.

Now go close some loops.
